DID YOU KNOW?

Gorillas are the largest living primates and share about 98.3% of their DNA with humans. They live in family groups led by a dominant silverback male and communicate through a complex system of vocalizations, body language, and facial expressions.

Common name: Western Lowland Gorilla

Scientific name: Gorilla gorilla gorilla

Type: Primate

Size: 4 to 6 feet

Diet: Herbivore

Habitat: Tropical Forests

Range: Central Africa VIEW MAP ➔

Gorillas are highly intelligent animals that live in complex social groups. A family group, called a troop, is typically led by a dominant silverback male who protects the group and makes decisions about where to travel and eat. Gorillas spend most of their day foraging for food, eating leaves, stems, fruit, and occasionally insects. They construct new sleeping nests each night from branches and leaves. Despite their fearsome appearance, gorillas are generally gentle and peaceful animals. They are critically endangered due to habitat loss and poaching, making conservation efforts vital for their survival.
