DID YOU KNOW?

Ring-tailed lemurs are one of the most recognizable primates thanks to their boldly striped black-and-white tails. They are highly social animals that live in groups called troops and spend a significant amount of time sunbathing in a "yoga pose" to warm up in the morning.

Common name: Ring-tailed Lemur

Scientific name: Lemur catta

Type: Primate

Size: 15 to 18 inches

Diet: Omnivore

Habitat: Dry Forests & Bush

Range: Madagascar VIEW MAP ➔

Ring-tailed lemurs are among the most social of all lemur species, living in groups of up to 30 individuals. Unusually for primates, females are dominant over males in lemur society. They communicate using scent marking, vocalizations, and visual signals — their striped tails are held high during group travel to keep members together. Lemurs are endemic to Madagascar, meaning they are found nowhere else on Earth. Due to rapid deforestation and hunting, ring-tailed lemurs are listed as endangered, making conservation and captive breeding programs crucial to their survival.
