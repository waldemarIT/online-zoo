DID YOU KNOW?

Bald eagles are not actually bald — their name comes from an older meaning of the word "bald," which meant "white-headed." They are powerful fliers and can dive at speeds of up to 100 mph when hunting fish.

Common name: Bald Eagle

Scientific name: Haliaeetus leucocephalus

Type: Bird of Prey

Wingspan: 6 to 7.5 feet

Diet: Carnivore

Habitat: Forests & Coasts

Range: North America VIEW MAP ➔

Bald eagles are the national symbol of the United States and one of the most recognizable birds in North America. They are powerful hunters that feed primarily on fish, which they snatch from the water with their large, sharp talons. Bald eagles build the largest nests of any bird in North America — some nests can weigh over a ton after years of additions. They mate for life and return to the same nesting area year after year. Once endangered due to hunting and pesticide use, bald eagle populations have made a remarkable recovery thanks to conservation efforts.
